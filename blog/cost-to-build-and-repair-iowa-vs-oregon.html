What It Costs to Build and Repair a Home: Iowa vs Oregon

The same roof costs very different money depending on which state it sits in. Here is the gap, and what it means for whether you fix a house or sell it.

Published September 4, 2026 • By Black Ridge Contracting

We build and repair homes in Central Iowa. What a job costs here and what the same job costs on the coast are very different numbers, and the gap is wider than most homeowners expect.

So we put real figures next to each other. Iowa and Oregon make a useful comparison because they sit on opposite sides of the national average on nearly every line.

What It Costs to Build

New construction is the cleanest way to compare two markets, because it prices labor, materials, and regulation all at once.

  • Iowa: roughly $100 to $160 per square foot, with some sources putting the full range at $90 to $200 depending on finish level.
  • Oregon statewide: commonly cited at $170 to $400 per square foot, with the state sitting well above the national average.
  • Portland specifically: $200 to $500 per square foot, with production and semi-custom work landing around $250 to $400.

On a 2,000 square foot house that is roughly $200,000 to $320,000 in Iowa against $500,000 to $800,000 in Portland for a production or semi-custom build. Same house. Same square footage.

Permits Are Not a Rounding Error

Permit and municipal fees are where the gap gets startling. In Iowa, build permits commonly run a few thousand dollars. In Portland, permits and development fees are commonly quoted at $10,000 to $22,000.

That is a several-fold difference on a line item most homeowners never think about until they see it.

Why Oregon Costs More

Three things drive most of it. A skilled labor shortage bids up trade rates. Strict environmental and energy codes add engineering, inspection, and material requirements. And terrain matters more out there, since hillside lots need foundation and drainage work that a flat Iowa lot simply does not.

Materials run about 5 to 10 percent above the national average in Portland on top of all that.

Oregon does hand one advantage back. The state has no sales tax, which saves roughly 5 to 8 percent on every dollar of materials. With materials making up roughly half of a build, that works out to something like $10,000 to $15,000 back on a $400,000 house. It softens the gap without closing it.

What This Means for Repairs

Repair pricing follows the same curve as new construction, because it is the same trades and the same supply houses. Roofing labor alone runs about $40 to $80 per hour per worker nationally, and where a market sits in that range tracks closely with its overall construction cost.

So a roof replacement, a siding job, or a foundation repair that we quote in Ankeny can cost meaningfully more for the same scope in Portland or Bend. That is labor supply, code, and overhead priced into the bid.

Now Put Home Values Next to It

Here is where it gets interesting, and where it actually changes decisions.

  • Des Moines: average home value around $212,000, with a median sale price near $240,000 as of mid-2026.
  • Portland: average home value around $534,000, with a median sale near $536,000.

Portland homes are worth roughly two and a half times what Des Moines homes are worth, while costing something like two to three times as much to build or repair. On straightforward work like a roof or a siding job, those multiples are close enough that the repair-to-value math lands in a similar place in both markets.

Where it drifts against the Oregon owner is permit-heavy work. Foundation, structural, and anything that triggers full plan review carries fees that are several times higher there, and those do not scale with the value of the house.

The other difference is the absolute dollars. A $30,000 repair list on a $240,000 Des Moines house is a decision. A $67,000 repair list on a $536,000 Portland house is the same share of the value and a much larger check to write.

And Cost of Living on Top

The construction gap sits inside a broader one. Iowa runs about 10 percent below the national average cost of living, roughly $2,228 a month for a single person and $4,905 for a family of four. Oregon runs about 10 percent above, around $2,722 and $5,995 for the same households.

Narrow it to the metros and it widens. Des Moines sits near 15 percent below the national average; Portland sits near 15 percent above.

Incomes run higher in Oregon too, so this is not straightforwardly less money in the bank. What it does mean is that the fixed cost of a repair lands on a household already carrying higher housing, fuel, and grocery costs.

The Decision This Actually Drives

When a homeowner asks whether to fix a house or sell it as-is, the honest answer depends on the market it sits in.

We walked through that decision for Iowa homeowners specifically in renovate before listing or sell as-is. In a lower-cost market like Central Iowa, repairs are often worth doing. Labor is reasonable, permits are cheap, and the spend is a manageable share of the home's value. We tell most Iowa homeowners to fix what is failing and keep the house.

In a high-cost market, the calculus tightens. When a house needs a roof, a foundation repair, and a system replacement in the same year, and every one of those bids comes in at coastal rates with five-figure permits attached, the repair budget can outrun what the finished house will appraise for. Selling as-is starts to look like the sensible option.
