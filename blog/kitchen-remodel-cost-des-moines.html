The kitchen is where your family gathers, where meals happen, and for most homes in the Des Moines metro, it is the room that has the biggest impact on resale value. But before you start tearing out cabinets, you need to know what a kitchen remodel actually costs in Central Iowa in 2026.

We have completed kitchen remodels across Ankeny, Des Moines, Urbandale, West Des Moines, and Johnston. Here is an honest look at what you should expect to spend. broken down by budget level.

Kitchen Remodel Cost Breakdown: Budget, Mid-Range, and High-End

Budget Kitchen Remodel: $15,000 to $25,000

A budget-friendly kitchen remodel focuses on cosmetic updates that make a big visual impact without gutting the room. Think refacing or painting existing cabinets, installing new laminate countertops, updating hardware, adding a new backsplash, and swapping out light fixtures. You keep the existing layout and plumbing, which saves a lot of money.

This is a great option if your kitchen is functional but just looks dated. You can transform the feel of the room without a massive investment.

Mid-Range Kitchen Remodel: $25,000 to $50,000

This is where most Des Moines homeowners land. A mid-range remodel typically includes new semi-custom cabinets, quartz or granite countertops, a tile backsplash, new flooring, updated appliances, and improved lighting. You might also add a small island or reconfigure a section of the layout.

At this price point, you are getting a kitchen that looks and feels completely new. The materials are durable, the design is modern, and the investment pays off at resale.

High-End Kitchen Remodel: $50,000 to $100,000+

A high-end kitchen remodel is a full transformation. Custom cabinetry, premium natural stone countertops, professional-grade appliances, custom lighting, and often a complete layout change including moving plumbing and electrical. You might add a large island with seating, a walk-in pantry, or open the kitchen to an adjacent living area.

This level of remodel makes sense in higher-value homes where the kitchen needs to match the rest of the house. It is a big investment, but when done right, it becomes the centerpiece of your home.

What Drives Kitchen Remodel Costs the Most?

Not all kitchen upgrades cost the same. Here are the biggest factors that move the needle on your total budget:

  • Cabinets: This is usually the single biggest expense, accounting for 25 to 35 percent of your budget. Stock cabinets are the cheapest, semi-custom cabinets are the sweet spot, and fully custom cabinets are the premium option.
  • Countertops: Laminate starts around $10 to $25 per square foot. Quartz runs $50 to $100. Natural stone like granite or marble can go even higher.
  • Layout changes: Moving plumbing, gas lines, or electrical adds cost fast. If you can keep the sink, stove, and fridge in their current locations, you will save thousands.
  • Appliances: A basic appliance package runs $2,000 to $5,000. Professional-grade appliances can easily hit $10,000 to $20,000.
  • Labor: In the Des Moines metro, labor typically accounts for 30 to 40 percent of the total project cost.

What Kind of ROI Can You Expect?

Kitchen remodels consistently rank among the best home improvement investments. In the Des Moines metro, a mid-range kitchen remodel typically returns 60 to 80 percent of its cost at resale. That means a $40,000 remodel could add $24,000 to $32,000 to your home's value.

The key is not to over-improve. If your home is worth $300,000, putting $100,000 into the kitchen might not pay off. Match your remodel level to your home's value and your neighborhood.

How Long Does a Kitchen Remodel Take?

Here is a realistic timeline for each level:

  • Budget remodel: 2 to 4 weeks
  • Mid-range remodel: 6 to 10 weeks
  • High-end remodel: 10 to 16 weeks

Custom cabinets and special-order materials are the biggest timeline factors. We always give you a clear schedule before work begins so you can plan around the disruption.

Tips to Save Money on Your Kitchen Remodel

  • Keep the existing layout. Moving plumbing and electrical is expensive. If the current layout works, keep it.
  • Reface instead of replace cabinets. If your cabinet boxes are solid, new doors and hardware can save you 30 to 50 percent over new cabinets.
  • Choose quartz over natural stone. Quartz is durable, low-maintenance, and often cheaper than granite or marble.
  • Skip the appliance upgrade if yours still work. You can always swap appliances later. focus your budget on the things that are harder to change.
  • Get multiple quotes. Talk to at least two or three contractors to make sure you are getting fair pricing for your area.
