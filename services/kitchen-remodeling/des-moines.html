About a quarter of the homes in Des Moines went up before 1940, and almost half were built before 1960. That shapes a kitchen remodel here. In neighborhoods like Beaverdale, Drake, Sherman Hill and Highland Park, the kitchen was often built as a small separate room with few outlets.

So the first question on a Des Moines kitchen is what the house can support. Can the wall between the kitchen and dining room come out? Is the wiring ready for a range, a microwave and a dishwasher on their own circuits? We answer those before you pick a countertop, because they decide the budget.

The Work

What we plan for in an older kitchen

Walls you want to remove

The wall between an old kitchen and dining room may be holding up the floor above. If it is load-bearing, taking it out is structural work that needs a properly sized beam, often an engineer's design, and a building permit. We tell you which kind of wall it is before you plan a layout around it.

Wiring that meets today's code

Homes built before 1940 can still have knob-and-tube wiring or a small electrical service. A licensed electrician checks the panel and the kitchen circuits, and anything that has to be upgraded goes into your written scope. More on that in our older-home wiring guide.

Old supply and drain lines

Galvanized steel supply pipes and cast iron drains are common in older houses. While the walls are open, a licensed plumber replaces what the remodel touches, so new cabinets are not sitting over decades-old pipe.

Cabinets for small rooms

A galley kitchen in a 1920s bungalow has no space to waste. We measure for cabinets that use the full wall and the full ceiling height, and for countertops cut to walls that are rarely square.

Floors that are not level

Old floor framing settles. Before tile or plank goes down, the subfloor is checked and flattened, because new flooring laid over a dip will crack or separate.

Plaster and original trim

Plaster cracks when it is cut carelessly. Where the remodel meets original plaster and wood trim, the patch is made to match what is already there.

Permits

Kitchen permits in Des Moines

Permits in the city go through the Des Moines Permit and Development Center at 1200 Locust Street, (515) 283-4200. On the city's 2026 fee schedule, a renovation permit for a house is $154.50. Electrical and mechanical permits for work on an existing home are $77.25 each, and plumbing work takes its own permit.

New cabinets alone are finish work. A new sink, disposal, circuit, gas line or wall change brings in permits. We confirm what your project needs with the permit office before your price is final, and the permit cost is in that price.

Cost

What a kitchen remodel costs in Des Moines

According to the Remodeling 2025 Cost vs. Value Report (www.costvsvalue.com), a midrange minor kitchen remodel in the Des Moines market averages $26,632 and adds about $22,762 at resale. That project keeps the layout of a 200-square-foot kitchen and replaces the cabinet fronts, countertops, appliances, sink and flooring.

A midrange major remodel, with all new semi-custom cabinets and an island, averages $77,852 and adds about $30,099.

An older house that needs new wiring or plumbing can run higher than these averages. Your price comes from a written scope after we see the kitchen.
