Ankeny is one of the newest housing markets in Iowa. About two out of three homes here were built in 2000 or later, and the median home dates to 2008. Many newer Ankeny kitchens already have an open plan, enough outlets and plumbing in good shape.

What many of them still have is the builder's original package: stock cabinets, laminate or entry-level stone, a small island and basic lighting. A remodel here usually keeps the layout and replaces what you touch every day. That costs less than gutting an older kitchen, and more of the budget goes into things you can see.

The Work

Upgrades that suit a newer Ankeny kitchen

Reface or replace the cabinets

If the cabinet boxes are solid, new doors, drawer fronts and hardware change the room for a fraction of the cost of new cabinets. If the boxes are worn or the layout wastes space, we price full replacement next to it so you can compare.

Countertops

Quartz or granite can replace laminate without moving a pipe when the layout stays put. The tops are measured after the cabinets are set, so seams and overhangs land where they should.

A bigger island

An island sized for two stools can be replaced with a larger one. That can mean moving a floor outlet and patching the flooring underneath. Both are in the scope from the start.

Better lighting

Under-cabinet lights, pendants over the island and added recessed lights are the usual additions. A licensed electrician puts them on the right circuits, with a permit where the city requires one.

Flooring across the main floor

In an open plan, the kitchen floor runs into the dining and living areas. We work out where the new floor should stop, so you are not left with a seam in the middle of the room.

Pantry and drop zone

Walk-in pantries and the area by the garage door get better shelving, pull-outs and a counter for small appliances.

Permits

Kitchen permits in Ankeny

Ankeny's Community Planning and Building Department issues permits. The city requires one for work that physically changes or adds structure, and separate permits for electrical, plumbing and mechanical installations. Those trade permits are pulled by contractors licensed in that trade. See the City of Ankeny building information.

The city says residential plans take about five working days to review, no work can start until the permit is issued, and inspections need 48 hours' notice. A kitchen that keeps its layout may only need trade permits, such as an electrical permit for new lighting. We settle that with the city before your price is final. The city's main line is 515-965-6400.
