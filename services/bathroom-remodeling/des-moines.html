Almost half of the homes in Des Moines were built before 1960. In those houses the bathroom is often about five feet by eight, with a cast iron tub, a thick tile floor set in mortar and supply pipes that have been in the wall since the house was new.

That is why a bathroom remodel in Des Moines starts with the plumbing, the floor framing and the ventilation, and the finish choices come second.

The Work

What an older bathroom usually needs

Getting the old tub out

A cast iron tub is heavy and usually comes out in pieces. We plan for that, check the framing under it, and set the new tub or shower base on a floor that can carry it.

Supply and drain lines

When the wall is open, a licensed plumber replaces the old galvanized supply lines and worn drain connections the remodel reaches. The work is inspected before the wall is closed.

Tile floors and floor height

Old mortar-bed tile is thick. Taking it out changes the floor height, so the new floor is built up to meet the hallway without a lip to trip on.

A real exhaust fan

Many older bathrooms rely on a window. We add a fan ducted to the outside, which keeps paint and drywall from failing in a room that sees steam every day.

Outlets and circuits

A bathroom should have ground-fault protected outlets on its own 20-amp circuit. A licensed electrician brings the room up to that while the walls are open.

Making a small room work

You usually cannot make a five-by-eight room bigger, so we use it better: a narrow or wall-hung vanity, a recessed medicine cabinet, a niche in the shower wall and a door that does not swing into the room.

Permits

Bathroom permits in Des Moines

Bathroom work in the city is permitted by the Des Moines Permit and Development Center, which sits in the city administration building at 1200 Locust Street and takes applications through its online portal. The office number is (515) 283-4200. Its 2026 fees list $154.50 for a renovation permit on a house and $77.25 apiece for electrical and mechanical permits on an existing home. Plumbing is permitted separately.

Des Moines requires a plumbing permit to install, alter or replace plumbing. Maintenance such as fixing a leak or repairing a faucet is exempt. Electrical and mechanical work, including a new exhaust fan, is permitted separately. We confirm what your project needs with the permit office and include the fees in your price.

Cost

What a bathroom remodel costs in Des Moines

According to the Remodeling 2025 Cost vs. Value Report (www.costvsvalue.com), a midrange bathroom remodel in the Des Moines market averages $24,330 and adds about $14,231 at resale. That project updates an existing 5-by-7-foot bathroom and replaces all the fixtures.

An upscale remodel, which expands a 35-square-foot bathroom to 100 square feet and relocates every fixture, averages $77,292 and adds about $26,778.

Replacing old supply and drain lines adds to these averages in a house that needs it. You get a fixed written price after we see the room and the plumbing below it.
