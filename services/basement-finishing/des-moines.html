A quarter of Des Moines homes were built before 1940. Their basements were made for a furnace, a coal bin and laundry, with no thought of anyone living down there. Ceilings can be low, walls can be stone, brick or early concrete block, and it is common to find signs of past water.

Those basements can still be finished once three things are true: there is enough headroom, the space stays dry, and there is a safe way out in an emergency. If one is missing, we tell you what it takes to fix and who does that work.

The Work

What we check before we price it

Headroom

The residential code calls for a seven-foot ceiling in finished living space. Ducts and beams that hang lower can sometimes be boxed in or rerouted.

Moisture comes first

Finishing over a damp wall traps the problem behind drywall. We look for stains, white mineral deposits and musty framing. If the basement needs drainage or foundation repair, a waterproofing specialist does that before we start. It is not work we do.

A way out

A basement bedroom needs an emergency escape window or door, and some cities require one for any new living space. If your plan needs one and the basement does not have it, an egress window contractor installs it first. We do not cut foundation walls.

Radon

The EPA places every county in Iowa in its highest radon zone. Test before you finish. If the level is high, a mitigation system goes in while the walls are still open.

Walls that suit old masonry

Framed walls are held off the foundation, set on a treated bottom plate and insulated in a way that suits a wall below ground.

Power, heat and alarms

Finished rooms need protected outlets, heat and smoke alarms. A basement bathroom needs its own vented fan. Licensed trades do each part under their own permit.

Permits

Basement permits in Des Moines

A basement finish in Des Moines needs a building permit. You apply through the Des Moines Permit and Development Center (1200 Locust Street, (515) 283-4200). Under the city's 2026 fees, the renovation permit for a house costs $154.50. The electrician and the heating contractor each pull a $77.25 permit for their part, and the plumber pulls one for a bathroom or bar sink.

The permit office reviews your plan against the building code, including ceiling height and emergency escape. We draw the plan, submit it and schedule the inspections, and those costs are in your price.

Cost

What basement finishing costs in Des Moines

According to the Remodeling 2025 Cost vs. Value Report (www.costvsvalue.com), a basement remodel in the Des Moines market averages $46,255 and adds about $34,374 at resale, which is about 74 cents on the dollar. That project finishes a 20-by-30-foot entertaining area with a wet bar and a 5-by-8-foot full bathroom.

Egress windows, drainage work and radon mitigation are separate costs when an older basement needs them, and they are done by specialists. We price our part in writing after a visit.
