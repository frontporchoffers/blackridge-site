About two out of three Ankeny homes were built in 2000 or later. Basements from those years usually have poured concrete walls and good headroom, and many came with an egress window and drain lines roughed in for a future bathroom.

The city spells out its requirements in a published guideline. We build to that list, pull the permits and schedule the inspections.

The Work

What the city requires, and how we build it

A floor plan for the city

Ankeny reviews a floor plan before it issues the permit: existing and new walls, what each room is for, dimensions and window sizes. We draw it and submit it.

An escape window

The city requires at least one emergency escape window or exterior door for new living space and for every bedroom, with a clear opening of at least 5.7 square feet. If your basement lacks one, an egress window contractor installs it before we start. We do not cut foundation walls.

Ceiling height and stairs

Finished ceilings have to be at least seven feet. If the space under the stairs is enclosed and can be reached, it has to be drywalled.

Framing and insulation

Bottom plates that touch the concrete are treated lumber. Walls are insulated to one of the city's listed options, such as R-19 in the stud cavity, with no vapor barrier on framed walls below ground.

Outlets and smoke alarms

Every basement outlet is ground-fault protected. Bedrooms and living areas get arc-fault protection, and a bedroom needs a hardwired smoke detector inside the room and outside it. A licensed electrician does this under an electrical permit.

Bathroom and furnace room

A basement bathroom needs an exhaust fan vented outside and its own 20-amp protected outlet. The furnace room keeps the clearances and combustion air the city requires.

Permits

Permits and inspections in Ankeny

A basement finish in Ankeny needs a building permit plus separate electrical, plumbing and mechanical permits for that work, pulled by contractors licensed in each trade. The requirements above come from Ankeny's Basement Finish guidelines.

The city inspects the rough-in electrical, plumbing and mechanical work and the framing before insulation or drywall goes up, then does a final inspection. A Certificate of Occupancy is required before the space is used. Allow about a week for the city to review the plan. Inspections are booked 48 hours ahead.
